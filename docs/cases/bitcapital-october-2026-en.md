## 1. Executive Summary

October 2026 marked an important transitional stage in the development of BitCapital.

During this reporting stage, four active peripheral pools completed their respective reporting periods: Rita, Ivan, Yulia, and Leonid.

All four pools recorded positive financial results.

Their combined profit amounted to **10,162.08 USDT**.

The combined starting capital base of the four reporting periods was 148,192 USDT. The ratio of aggregate profit to this base was 6.86%.

Because the reporting periods had different starting and closing dates, this figure does not represent the return of a single calendar month.

The principal organizational development was the completion of the transition of all four peripheral pools to the annuity model.

By November 5, 2026:

- All four pools had completed their respective reporting periods.
- Rita, Ivan, and Leonid had completed their first annuity periods.
- Yulia had completed her October period under the previous profit-distribution model.
- Yulia's capital base for the next period had been increased to 50,000 USDT.
- All four pools had entered the annuity architecture.
- The combined nominal capital base of the new configuration amounted to 170,000 USDT.

The transition was completed without requiring automatic liquidation of the residual pools.

## 2. Operational Architecture

BitCapital is an active applied project that uses research results from Wave Liquidity Redistribution Theory (WLRT) and the analytical capabilities of WaveCounter.

As of the reporting date, BitCapital was not incorporated as a separate legal entity.

Relationships among participants were organized through separate bilateral agreements defining their respective functions, authority, and responsibilities.

The system distinguishes three principal functions.

### Investor

The Investor owns the assets and participates in an experimental scientific pool.

The Investor authorizes the Pool Operator to manage the relevant pool.

### BitCapital

BitCapital performs an analytical and informational function.

The project produces informational tickets containing, among other parameters:

- The diagnosed state of the market environment.
- Permissible asset allocation ranges.
- Parameters required to assess the pool's condition.

An informational ticket does not constitute an instruction to execute a specific transaction.

BitCapital does not directly manage the Investor's assets.

### Pool Operator

The Pool Operator manages the pool under the authority granted by the Investor.

Using the information provided in BitCapital tickets, the Operator independently determines the necessary transactions, their amounts, timing, and execution methods to bring the asset allocation within permissible ranges.

The specific rights, obligations, and responsibilities of participants are governed by their respective agreements and authorizations.

## 10. Economic Logic of the Transition

The annuity model changes the settlement mechanism between participants and the system.

Under the previous model, the completion of a reporting period was followed by profit distribution.

Under the annuity model, the participant receives a scheduled payment comprising an interest component and partial repayment of principal.

At the same time, the residual pool may continue its economic activity.

A central feature of the new architecture is the separation of:

- Outstanding obligations to the participant.
- Actual composition of pool assets.
- Economic ownership interests within the pool.
- Profit available for distribution.
- Liquidity required for the next payment.
- Reserves supporting future obligations.

An annuity payment does not automatically require liquidation of the pool.

If current obligations can be fulfilled using available liquidity, other assets need not be sold.

Where liquidity is insufficient, assets may be sold to the extent necessary to meet the obligations, subject to the Operator's authority and applicable constraints.

## 11. Reserves and System Sustainability

The transition to the annuity model creates a new structure of recurring payment obligations.

Consequently, the system's sustainability depends not only on asset returns but also on its ability to provide sufficient payment liquidity when obligations become due.

Key monitoring areas include:

1. Liquidity availability on scheduled annuity payment dates.
2. Formation and utilization of reserves.
3. Continued operational capacity of residual pools.
4. Changes in outstanding obligations to participants.
5. Formation of economic ownership interests within pools.
6. Capacity to continue operating without additional external capital.

Reserves are treated as an adaptive mechanism for supporting payment obligations.

Reserve formation does not necessarily require permanent withdrawal of the corresponding assets from productive activity.

The actual reserve balances across all four pools have not been quantitatively verified for this report.

## 12. Research Significance

The October transition provides an applied case of organizational adaptation within a liquidity redistribution system.

From the perspective of WLRT, the following aspects are particularly relevant:

- Changes in the structure of obligations while maintaining active pools.
- Separation of financial performance from cash distributions.
- Preservation of the capacity for subsequent redistribution.
- Changes in economic ownership interests within pools.
- Relationships among liquidity, permissible actions, and system sustainability.
- The role of informational market diagnostics in independent pool management by Operators.

WaveCounter serves as an applied analytical instrument.

BitCapital provides informational tickets, while actual asset management is performed by Operators under authority granted by Investors.

The observed results establish that the corresponding reporting periods closed with positive returns.

They do not, by themselves, establish future profitability, long-term sustainability of the annuity model, or guaranteed fulfillment of all obligations.

These properties require further investigation across subsequent periods.

## 13. Conclusions

The October 2026 transitional report records five principal results.

**First.** All four peripheral pools completed their respective reporting periods with positive profits.

**Second.** The combined result amounted to **+10,162.08 USDT**, equivalent to **+6.86%** of the combined starting capital bases.

**Third.** Rita, Ivan, and Leonid completed their first annuity periods. Their combined scheduled payments amounted to **6,344.53 USDT**, including **3,944.53 USDT** in principal repayments.

**Fourth.** Yulia completed October under the previous profit-distribution model and established a new pool with a 50,000 USDT capital base for the annuity model.

**Fifth.** By November 5, all four peripheral pools had entered the annuity architecture, with a combined nominal capital base of **170,000 USDT**.

October 2026 therefore represents the transition from the previous periodic profit-distribution model to a system of recurring annuity obligations while preserving the continued operation of residual pools.

The next research stage will focus on evaluating the sustainability of this architecture, reserve formation, changes in economic ownership structures, and the system's capacity to meet obligations without additional external capital.
