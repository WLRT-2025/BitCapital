# BitCapital. August 2026 Results

## Four Pools as a Unified Liquidity Management System

August 2026 became an important practical stage for BitCapital. By September 5, full management cycles had been completed for four Pools: Ivan, Rita, Yulia, and Leonid. They differed in size, start date, initial asset composition, and management trajectory.

That difference proved fundamental.

At first glance, one might expect four Pools managed under the same methodology to gradually converge toward approximately the same structure. August demonstrated the opposite.

**One management system does not imply identical portfolios.**

Each Pool had its own starting point, its own history of previous operations, its own settlement date, its own proportions of USDT, KEFEN, BTC, and SOL, and therefore its own available capacity for further redistribution.

This is one of the clearest practical manifestations of **Wave Liquidity Redistribution Theory (WLRT)**.

---

## 1. What Was Actually Being Managed

In conventional portfolio logic, the central question is often formulated approximately as follows:

> Which assets should be bought or sold now?

In BitCapital's practical model, the question is different:

> What state is the Pool in now, and which liquidity redistributions are available from that state?

Asset prices remain important, but they are no longer the sole center of decision-making.

A Pool is treated as a changing system:

**X(t) = (USDT, KEFEN, BTC, SOL, ...)**

Yet a list of assets alone is not sufficient. What also matters is **Redistribution Capability** — the Pool's ability to move from its current state into other admissible states.

In the broader language of WLRT research, this is associated with the variable **R**: a representation of the currently available redistribution possibilities.

Two Pools can therefore operate in the same market, observe the same BTC, SOL, and KEFEN prices, and still require completely different actions.

That is not a flaw in the system.

**That is the system.**

It is important not to interpret 11.39% as the conventional monthly return of a single fund for calendar August. The Pools began and ended their periods on different dates. This is an aggregated result of four separate completed management cycles.

That distinction is important for the entire BitCapital architecture.

## 3. Ivan's Pool: Releasing Working Liquidity

Ivan's period began on July 29.

The starting value was approximately **10,005.83 USDT**, while only about **872 USDT** remained as free USDT liquidity after the previous settlement.

A significant part of the Pool was held in KEFEN, BTC, and SOL.

The management task was therefore not simply to wait for asset appreciation. It was necessary to gradually increase the freedom available for subsequent actions.

During the period, portions of SOL and BTC were converted into USDT.

In WLRT terms, this is an example of **restoring Redistribution Capability**.

Selling an asset can serve two functions simultaneously:

1. realizing part of the result;
2. converting bound liquidity back into liquidity that can be redirected toward several possible future states.

By August 29, the Pool value had reached **11,557.58 USDT**.

The result for the period was:

**+1,551.75 USDT**, or **+15.51%**.

After profit is separated, the Pool's capital returns approximately to its original period base, but it is **not the same state** that existed one month earlier.

The redistribution history has changed.

Prices have changed.

The internal proportions of assets have changed.

And the Pool's capacity to respond to subsequent market changes has changed.

## 4. Rita's Pool: Managing Deviation and Subsequent Finalization

Rita's Pool proved particularly useful from a research perspective.

At the start of the period on July 26, its structure was:

- USDT — 3,022;
- KEFEN — 19,820,000;
- BTC — 0.0727;
- SOL — 13.679.

Starting value:

**20,002.48 USDT.**

An important practical episode occurred during the period. While executing an operation, the operator moved part of the funds into SOL instead of performing the intended KEFEN → USDT redistribution.

In a conventional model, this could simply be treated as a transaction error.

Within the BitCapital architecture, the situation can be understood more broadly.

**The state of the Pool changed.**

Consequently, the set of available subsequent actions changed as well.

Rather than attempting to mentally “undo” the previous event, a corrective ticket was issued: the excess SOL was converted back into USDT.

This illustrates an important practical principle.

WaveCounter™ is not intended to claim that a system will never deviate from a plan. Its value is greater if it can identify:

**where the system actually is now and which next transition restores the required structure.**

Additional BTC and KEFEN redistributions followed.

On the closing date, August 26, the Pool value was:

**22,313.95 USDT.**

Result:

**+2,311.47 USDT**, or **+11.56%**.

A separate **account finalization** process then began.

Instead of one large market sale of KEFEN, a sequence of internal KEFEN ↔ USDT exchanges was used between wallets and Pools at rates fixed at the time of each transaction.

The remaining BTC position was also fully closed.

Thus, **period closing** and **Pool finalization** emerged as two distinct processes.

The first records the economic result of the period.

The second transforms the physical distribution of assets after that result has been determined.

## 5. Yulia's Pool: An Example of Active Redistribution

Yulia's Pool began its period on August 1 with a value of:

**28,192.59 USDT.**

Initial structure:

- USDT — 12,139;
- KEFEN — 15,500,000;
- BTC — 0.06567;
- SOL — 37.92852.

During the month, the Pool passed through several successive states.

Part of USDT was first redistributed into BTC.

Later, part of SOL was returned to USDT.

BTC and SOL positions were subsequently reduced.

Near the end of the period, the direction changed again: part of the accumulated USDT was redistributed back into BTC and SOL.

A particularly illustrative operation was the **internal acquisition of KEFEN from Rita's Pool for 3,000 USDT**.

The same transaction was useful for both Pools.

Rita needed to reduce KEFEN and increase USDT.

Yulia, in contrast, needed to increase KEFEN and deploy excess USDT.

What represents an exit requirement for one Pool can become an appropriate entry point for another.

This is no longer simply the management of several independent wallets.

An **internal BitCapital liquidity redistribution network** begins to appear.

Instead of only:

**Pool → external market → Pool**

an additional route becomes available:

**Pool A → Pool B**

with the external market used where internal redistribution does not solve the task.

Yulia's period closed on September 1 at:

**30,826.28 USDT.**

Profit:

**2,633.68 USDT.**

Return:

**9.34%**.

After the result is separated, the capital base again returns to approximately its initial 28.2 thousand USDT, but the Pool's structure is substantially different from its starting structure in August.

## 6. Leonid's Pool: The Largest of the Four

Leonid's Pool began its period on August 5.

Starting value:

**45,587 USDT.**

Composition:

- USDT — 16,779;
- KEFEN — 38,400,000;
- BTC — 0.0685;
- SOL — 22.255.

During the period, part of USDT was progressively allocated into BTC and SOL.

Later, as the market environment and the internal state of the Pool changed, the redistribution direction reversed: portions of BTC and SOL were converted back into USDT.

This produced another important conclusion from August practice.

**The direction of an operation is not a permanent property of an asset.**

BTC is not simply an asset that we “buy.”

USDT is not simply an asset that we “accumulate.”

SOL is not simply an asset that we “hold.”

In a particular state, one asset may serve as a source of liquidity while another becomes its recipient.

Several days later, those roles may reverse.

By September 5, Leonid's Pool consisted of:

- USDT — 17,069;
- KEFEN — 38,400,000;
- BTC — 0.07386;
- SOL — 32.7568.

Total value:

**50,909.65 USDT.**

Profit:

**5,322.65 USDT.**

Return:

**11.68%**.

The quantity of KEFEN remained almost unchanged throughout the period.

A significant part of the result arose from the change in value of the existing position while BTC, SOL, and USDT were actively managed around it.

Redistribution does not necessarily mean continuous movement of every component in the system.

Sometimes the function of one element is precisely to **remain relatively stable** while working liquidity is redistributed around it.

## 7. Why the Four Pools Should Not Look Identical

August provided a practical answer to a fundamental question in the BitCapital architecture.

Imagine two Pools.

One begins with 10,000 USDT.

Another with 50,000 USDT.

One started on July 29.

The other on August 5.

One has already reduced BTC.

The other has just increased BTC.

One has accumulated excess USDT.

The other has a significant amount of liquidity bound in KEFEN.

Even if both are managed under the same system and observe the same market, **their correct next action does not have to be identical**.

Simple trade copying would therefore undermine the logic of adaptive management itself.

The same strategy does not mean the same action.

It means **the same rules for determining an action from the current state**.

## 8. From Calendar Time to Phase Time

Another feature became visible because the Pools have different settlement dates.

Rita closes on the 26th.

Ivan on the 29th.

Yulia on the 1st.

Leonid on the 5th.

The phrase “August result” therefore no longer fully describes what is happening.

Each Pool has its own cycle.

Two Pools can exist on the same calendar date while functionally occupying different stages.

One may be accumulating working liquidity.

Another may be redistributing.

A third may be realizing the period result.

A fourth may be in finalization and preparation for the next cycle.

This corresponds to the concept of **Phase Time** developed within WLRT.

Calendar time alone is not sufficient to describe the state of an adaptive system.

We also need to know **where the system is within its own cycle**.

The four August Pools provided a practical working example of this principle.

## 9. The Role of KEFEN

August also made KEFEN's practical role inside BitCapital clearer.

If KEFEN is viewed only as a market token, part of the system's behavior is difficult to explain.

Within the Pool architecture, KEFEN increasingly performs an additional function — that of a **redistribution asset**.

It can:

- remain stable in one Pool;
- be reduced in another;
- move between Pools;
- be exchanged for USDT inside the system;
- help restore a required liquidity balance without mandatory use of the external market.

The interaction between Rita's and Yulia's Pools demonstrated this particularly clearly.

This does not mean that KEFEN automatically becomes a universal settlement instrument for the entire system. Such a conclusion would be premature.

But August demonstrated that its functionality is already broader than that of a conventional speculative position.

## 11. Tickets as Part of the Model

August also clarified the role of BitCapital's ticket system.

At first glance, a ticket is simply an operational instruction.

In practice, it performs a more important function.

Each ticket records:

**state → admissible action → expected next state.**

This creates a sequence:

**X0 → X1 → X2 → ... → Xn**

The history of a Pool therefore becomes more than a list of transactions.

It becomes a **trajectory of states**.

A working ticket records a transition within a period.

A period-closing ticket records a cycle boundary.

A finalization ticket records a transformation after the result has been determined.

The appearance of cross-wallet forming operations also indicates that an additional level is emerging — operations that belong to the architecture of the entire system rather than to one particular Pool.

This is a natural development of the infrastructure.

The financial results of the four Pools are important.

Liquidity can be redistributed not only among assets within a Pool, but also between Pools.

KEFEN begins to function as an internal redistribution element.

Tickets evolve from instructions into a journal of system transitions.

And WaveCounter™ gains a clearer future function — not to predict price, but to **observe, compare, and coordinate the states of an adaptive liquidity network**.

August was therefore more than another profitable month.

It was the month in which BitCapital's practical activity began to take a form directly consistent with the theoretical architecture of Wave Liquidity Redistribution Theory (WLRT).
